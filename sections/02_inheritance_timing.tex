\section{Sprint candidate 2: Timing of parental inheritance}
\label{sec:timing}

\subsection{Five-day question}
At what age do people report receiving parental inheritances, and how closely does the second parent's death approximate receipt timing? The comparison is entirely within an authorised longitudinal survey.

\subsection{Data package by December 2026}
\textbf{ELSA (UKDS 5050; Waves 0--11, 1998--2024)} is currently available under End User Licence. Prepare a documented, licence-compliant extract with respondent birth year/age, parental survival or death years, inheritance receipt year/source/value, cohort, socioeconomic measures, survey weights and longitudinal identifiers. Wave 6 inheritance histories are an initial lead; exact variable names and overlapping observations must be confirmed.

For respondents with both measures, construct:
\[
a_{\mathrm{receipt}}=t_{\mathrm{receipt}}-t_{\mathrm{birth}},
\quad
a_{\mathrm{second\ death}}=\max(t_{\mathrm{mother}},t_{\mathrm{father}})-t_{\mathrm{birth}},
\quad
\Delta a=a_{\mathrm{receipt}}-a_{\mathrm{second\ death}}.
\]
Inheritance can precede the last parental death, follow it with delay, or never occur.

\subsection{Five-day deliverables}
Age-at-receipt and age-at-second-death distributions; their overlap and differences; cohort/socioeconomic comparisons; missingness, recall-error and right-censoring diagnostics; reproducible code and disclosure-safe tables. The sprint is conditional on approved participant access and usable variable overlap, rather than merely the existence of the dataset.

\subsection{To do by 31 December 2026}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Obtain ELSA EUL files and questionnaires; identify the exact timing variables.
\item Check overlap sample sizes, parents alive, death before panel entry and survey weights.
\item Build a minimal analysis extract and produce preliminary distributions.
\item Confirm Turing participant access and permitted computing arrangements.
\item Apply a go/no-go check: if access or overlap is insufficient, do not promise empirical sprint estimates.
\item Make sure data sprint participants have access to the approved datasets, documentation and required computing environment before the sprint.
\item Prepare documents for data sprint participants explaining the tasks, suggested teams, division of work, coordination points and possible expansions or stretch goals.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Participant preparation and suggested teams}
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[Access preflight] Have a prospective participant confirm ELSA EUL permission, approved Turing environment, the exact variables and sufficient overlap; do not circulate raw survey extracts outside permitted arrangements.
\item[Participant brief] Explain age at receipt, second-parent-death proxy, sample construction, censoring and the limits of bereavement comparisons. Include a variable map and starter sample-flow code.
\item[Suggested teams] Team A builds inheritance histories and receipt ages; Team B builds parental-death histories and censoring checks. Combine them to quantify proxy errors and cohort differences.
\item[Stretch goals] Heterogeneity by parental or recipient socioeconomic position and descriptive wealth outcomes where repeated events suffice.
\end{description}

\subsection{Longer-term extension}
Extend to Understanding Society or WAS where timing variables allow; examine wealth, housing and retirement trajectories around receipt and parental death; incorporate richer family background and administrative death or estate records only through separately authorised routes. Bereavement and inheritance effects must remain conceptually distinct. Anonymised survey respondents must never be reverse-identified against probate records.
