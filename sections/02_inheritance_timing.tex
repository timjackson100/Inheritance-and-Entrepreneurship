\section{Strand 2: When do people inherit?}
\label{sec:timing}

\subsection{Aim and primary data}
Estimate age at reported parental inheritance and test whether age at the second parent's death is a useful proxy for inheritance timing.

The primary source is \textbf{ELSA (UKDS 5050)}, particularly candidate Wave 6 inheritance histories and parental survival/death questions. The required fields are respondent birth year, inheritance year/source/value, both parents' death years or survival status, cohort, socioeconomic characteristics, survey weights and longitudinal ID. Exact variable names, accessible waves and overlap remain to be verified.

\subsection{Timing measures}
For respondents with suitable records:
\[
a_{\mathrm{receipt}}=t_{\mathrm{receipt}}-t_{\mathrm{birth}},
\qquad
a_{\mathrm{second\ death}}
=\max(t_{\mathrm{mother}},t_{\mathrm{father}})-t_{\mathrm{birth}}.
\]
Compare their distributions and calculate
\[
\Delta a=a_{\mathrm{receipt}}-a_{\mathrm{second\ death}}
\]
only where both measures are observed. An inheritance may arrive at the first death, before either death, after a delay or not at all; the proxy is therefore a hypothesis to test.

\subsection{Empirical work and outputs}
Audit sample overlap, missingness and censoring; estimate age distributions and receipt--death differences by cohort and socioeconomic group. Account for recall error, parents still alive and deaths preceding panel entry. Where events and panel observations permit, describe changes in wealth, housing, employment and retirement, while separating inheritance receipt from bereavement effects.

Outputs: variable inventory, sample-flow table, age and proxy-validation estimates, uncertainty/sensitivity checks, and shareable code with non-disclosive results.

\subsection{Extensions and access}
WAS (UKDS 7215) can independently analyse receipts and wealth; Understanding Society (UKDS 6614) may support replication if suitable variables exist. Public ONS life tables can benchmark mortality patterns. These are not guaranteed DSG datasets.

ELSA access and Turing hosting must comply with UK Data Service terms. Survey respondents must not be identified or matched to named probate records. If suitable data access or variables are unavailable, deliver the variable audit, protocol and synthetic test code rather than unsupported empirical estimates.
