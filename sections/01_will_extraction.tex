\section{Strand 1: Extracting inheritance information from wills}
\label{sec:wills}

\subsection{Research question}
How accurately can an open-weight language model, adapted from US will-extraction benchmarks, identify people, family relationships, assets and intended bequests in wills from England and Wales?

The immediate data objective is a structured, source-grounded representation of what a will \emph{intends} to transfer. A bequest in a will is not evidence that a beneficiary received it.

\subsection{Data inventory}
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[IE4Wills (core benchmark)]
A published dataset of 45 authentic US wills and 16,018 human annotations covering testators, beneficiaries, assets, relationships and bequests. Its established annotation scheme permits a reproducible extraction baseline. Use is subject to the CC BY-NC 4.0 licence and an event-use rights check.
\item[Modern wills from England and Wales (conditional)]
A proposed, independently reviewed sample of 10--20 probate wills, subject to purchase, lawful processing, participant access, hosting and annotation. It tests transfer from US to UK drafting practice; it is too small to support representative claims about national extraction accuracy.
\end{description}

Scotland and Northern Ireland are separate potential extensions, not part of the initial UK-will benchmark.

\subsection{Target variables}
For each document, extract and preserve the relevant source passage for:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the testator and any executors or trustees;
\item named beneficiaries and stated family or other relationships;
\item assets, specific gifts, residuary shares and conditional bequests;
\item gift conditions and contingent beneficiaries;
\item uncertainty, ambiguity, absent information and failed extractions.
\end{itemize}
The representation must distinguish a stated relationship from a relationship inferred by the model and must avoid inventing missing beneficiaries or transfers.

\subsection{Data and evaluation workflow}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Check the IE4Wills licence, obtain its documents and published data splits, and reproduce a conventional or published extraction baseline.
\item Define a consistent structured output schema, with evidence spans for every extracted assertion.
\item Obtain and review the permitted England-and-Wales sample, if approved; create independent reference labels.
\item Compare unadapted US-trained/prompted extraction with UK-specific prompts and schema adaptation. Fine-tuning is optional and requires enough authorised examples.
\item Score entity/relation precision, recall and F1, evidence-span accuracy, unsupported assertions, uncertainty and computational cost. Report US and UK performance separately.
\end{enumerate}

The published IE4Wills test split is small, so uncertainty should be reported at document level. Placeholder names in IE4Wills do not establish real-world identity-linkage accuracy.

\subsection{Expected outputs}
A documented extraction schema; clean benchmark and scoring scripts; runnable open-weight baselines; an error taxonomy; and a report on what UK-specific adaptation does and does not achieve.

\subsection{Alternative routes to a UK wills corpus}
The original proposal relied on purchasing a small number of post-probate wills through HMCTS. We should also seek a research partnership with holders of modern wills and estate records. These routes yield different information and should not be treated as interchangeable.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[HMCTS probate records: public baseline]
Purchase post-grant copies to build an independent, reproducible test sample across drafting styles and estate sizes. Public probate copies are a baseline, not a complete record of all deaths or a statement of actual distributions. The will is private before a grant of probate; access and processing conditions still matter.
\item[Will-writing platforms: drafted text and metadata]
Approach services such as Farewill (now part of Dignity) and Co-op Legal Services, which provide will-writing services and may hold digital will documents. Ask whether they would support a controlled academic pilot with consent or another appropriately established legal basis, for example by running extraction inside the provider's environment and sharing only aggregate accuracy measures. Draft wills may have been amended, revoked, or never proved: they cannot be interpreted as realised inheritances.
\item[Solicitors and private-client departments]
Approach both specialist private-client firms and regional practices. They may offer diverse legal drafting, trusts, complex conditions and professionally verified reference labels. A small set of collaborating firms risks strong selection by client wealth and estate complexity; record the sampling frame and avoid population claims.
\item[Estate-administration and probate providers]
Prioritise providers such as Co-op Legal Services and Kings Court Trust for a separate question: whether closed estate files contain final estate accounts, distributions, dates and net receipts. These are substantially more valuable than wills alone for studying realised transfers. Verify what is actually retained and whether access is lawful before designing linkage.
\item[Will registries and search services]
The National Will Register and Estatesearch may assist with metadata, coverage, locating a current will or identifying a potential partner network. A registration/search database is \emph{not} necessarily a repository of the full will text or a beneficiary-level transfer dataset.
\item[Probate genealogists and beneficiary-tracing firms]
Firms such as Fraser \& Fraser may have information on tracing beneficiaries and intestacies; their role is different from a general source of wills or completed distributions. These partnerships would primarily test identification and missing-heir issues, subject to rights and privacy.
\end{description}

\subsection{What to ask potential partners}
Send a short research scoping enquiry, rather than asking for a bulk export. Request:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Which records are held: drafts, signed final wills, probate copies, codicils, estate inventories, final accounts, distribution ledgers, beneficiary relationships and payment dates?
\item Approximate number of cases, years, England/Wales coverage, digitisation/OCR quality, completeness and retention periods.
\item Whether an estate is linked internally to a final version of the will and to actual distributions; whether an executed will can be distinguished from an unexecuted draft.
\item Whether a small, governed pilot is possible using redacted documents, a secure environment, provider-run extraction or derived annotation statistics.
\item What controller, client-confidentiality, privacy, ethics, intellectual-property, legal-basis and data-sharing approvals would be required.
\item Whether anonymised sample characteristics can be supplied to quantify selection bias relative to national probate/estate statistics.
\end{enumerate}

A useful first-stage deliverable is a \emph{provider feasibility matrix} recording document type, sample size, realised-receipt information, permissions, cost, representativeness and likely effort. Contact does not imply agreement or access. Do not upload identifiable wills to external AI services without explicit permission and a suitable agreement. Pseudonymised beneficiary records remain personal data.

\subsection{Preparation status and dependencies}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Available starting point:} public IE4Wills benchmark, subject to licence compliance.
\item \textbf{Not yet assured:} 10--20 lawfully usable UK wills and independently reviewed labels.
\item \textbf{Fallback:} report reproducible US benchmark results and the limitation on UK generalisation.
