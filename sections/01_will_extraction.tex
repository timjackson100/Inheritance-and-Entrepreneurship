\section{Strand 1: Extracting information from UK wills}
\label{sec:wills}

\subsection{Aim and core data}
Test whether open-weight AI can identify testators, beneficiaries, family relationships, assets and intended bequests in England-and-Wales wills, with an auditable source passage for every claim. \emph{A stated bequest is not a confirmed receipt.}

The initial benchmark is \textbf{IE4Wills}: 45 authentic US wills with 16,018 annotations (CC BY-NC 4.0; confirm intended use). A proposed sample of \textbf{10--20 UK wills}, subject to legal access and independent annotation, would test transfer to UK drafting. Scotland and Northern Ireland are separate extensions. This small UK sample permits a feasibility study, not a nationally representative accuracy estimate.

\subsection{Extraction and validation}
Extract the testator, executors/trustees, named beneficiaries, stated relationships, asset types, specific/residuary/conditional gifts and uncertainty. Compare an unadapted US baseline with UK-specific prompts and schema changes; fine-tuning is optional if enough authorised labels exist. Score entity/relation precision, recall and F1, supporting text spans, unsupported assertions and compute cost. Report UK performance separately from US results and use document-level uncertainty intervals.

\subsection{Routes to additional UK documents}
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[HMCTS probate copies]
Purchasable post-grant wills provide an independent test set, but do not show all deaths or actual transfers.
\item[Will-writing platforms]
Approach Farewill/Dignity and Co-op Legal Services about a secure research pilot on signed or digitally drafted wills. Distinguish valid final wills from drafts, revoked documents or wills never proved.
\item[Solicitors and private-client firms]
Potentially useful for complex drafting, trusts and manually checked annotations; samples will overrepresent their clients.
\item[Probate and estate-administration firms]
Co-op Legal Services and Kings Court Trust are promising contacts for \emph{completed estate accounts}, actual net distributions and payment dates. These records would enable research beyond textual extraction.
\item[Will registers/search services]
The National Will Register and Estatesearch may identify holders and metadata but should not be assumed to contain full will text or beneficiary receipts.
\item[Probate genealogists]
Firms such as Fraser \& Fraser could inform missing-heir and intestacy research, subject to access and confidentiality.
\end{description}

For each contact, establish the record types held (signed wills, codicils, estate accounts, distribution ledgers), case numbers and dates, retention, geographic coverage, cost, digitisation, research permissions and selection bias. A provider-run, secure extraction pilot with disclosure-safe outputs may be more realistic than receiving a bulk document export.

\subsection{Data conditions and outputs}
Modern wills can contain personal information about living people. Any use needs appropriate controller permissions, lawful basis, safeguards and a compliant computing environment; pseudonymised data remain personal data. No identifiable wills should be sent to an external model service without explicit permission.

Outputs: an extraction schema, a reproducible benchmark and scoring code, performance/error tables, and a provider-feasibility matrix. If UK access is not agreed, retain the US benchmark and explicitly report the generalisation limitation.
