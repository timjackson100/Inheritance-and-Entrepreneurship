\section{UK research stream C: Lifetime gifts, donor choices and estate distribution}
\label{sec:gifts}

An inheritance-data programme should distinguish the recipient-side impact of wealth from \emph{when and why donors transfer it}. Inter vivos gifts, loans, trusts, inheritance at death and charitable legacies are economically different records. Existing IFS work by Boileau and Sturrock on gifts and loans using WAS provides a starting point.

\subsection{C1. Lifetime giving versus inheritance at death}
\textbf{Question:} How much wealth is transferred during the giver's life rather than at death, and how do transfer amounts and recipient ages vary by donor wealth, relationship and cohort?

\textbf{Data needed:} dates/values and donor--recipient relationship for gifts \emph{given} and \emph{received}, loans and repayments, estate bequests, birth years and donor wealth. WAS is an initial source, but two-sided records from the same family would be a major improvement. Gift survey nonresponse and incomplete tax reporting need explicit treatment. Proposed WAS questionnaire changes must not be mistaken for variables currently available.

\subsection{C2. Transfers within families and unequal division}
\textbf{Question:} Are inheritances divided equally among children, and how do unequal wills and earlier lifetime gifts affect the total resources received by siblings?

\textbf{Data needed:} number of children, family relationships, lifetime gifts to each child, final will/codicil and final estate distributions (not merely the gross estate). Record trusts, spousal rights, property held jointly, intestacy, business assets, and bequests to non-children.

\textbf{First output:} distribution of intended and, where possible, realised sibling shares, and sensitivity of aggregate projections to equal-split assumptions. A sample from professional will writers is not population representative without weighting/validation.

\subsection{C3. Care costs, late-life wealth depletion and transfer timing}
\textbf{Question:} How much of the variation in terminal estate values and late gifts is associated with care needs, health changes, widowhood and uncertainty about longevity?

\textbf{Data needed:} repeated donor wealth by asset type; health, care and household status; care costs and transfers; death dates and eventual estate values. ELSA can potentially study older-household wealth paths; a donor-to-estate link would be a further approved project. Care costs must not be inferred from total wealth depletion alone.

\subsection{C4. Business succession and in-kind inheritance}
\textbf{Question:} What proportion of business wealth changes hands through inheritance rather than cash, and what happens to family firms after succession?

\textbf{Data needed:} estate asset classes, ownership before/after death, beneficiary relationship, business relief claims, company survival, investment and employment. Tax and firm-level administrative linkage would be essential for comprehensive evidence; a will's statement of shares is not proof of the final ownership change.

\subsection{C5. Tax, avoidance and estate planning}
\textbf{Question:} How do reliefs, thresholds and tax-rule changes affect lifetime giving, estate composition, the timing of transfers and eventual recipient distribution?

\textbf{Data needed:} reform dates, estate tax returns and relief claims, gifts with dates and relationship, asset composition, death and donor demographics. HMRC records only cover defined reportable/taxable subsets; non-taxable gifts may be poorly observed. Separate responses to tax from secular wealth and mortality trends.

\subsection{C6. Charitable legacies and non-family recipients}
\textbf{Question:} How much wealth is bequeathed to charities, friends, trusts and other non-lineal recipients, and does this differ by estate size and family circumstances?

\textbf{Data needed:} beneficiary type and residuary shares in wills, completed estate distributions, charitable legacy receipts and donor characteristics. Planned charitable gifts in wills may never become realised payments. A charity/provider partnership may offer aggregate validation without disclosing living beneficiaries.

\subsection{Proposed data products}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item A transfer-type dictionary distinguishing gifts, loans, trust benefits, probate inheritances, spousal transfers, business succession and charity.
\item A donor/recipient/timing feasibility matrix showing which data capture both sides of a transfer.
\item A validated sample of will allocation clauses versus final estate distribution records, with missingness and sampling restrictions.
\item A within-WAS descriptive panel of available giving and receiving measures, separated from any future questionnaire proposals.
\end{itemize}
