\begin{abstract}
Seven self-contained potential Data Study Group challenges, drawn from the 18 October 2026 Turing submission and subsequent UK inheritance work. Each section specifies a five-day research question, a data package to prepare by \textbf{31 December 2026}, measurable sprint outputs, preparation tasks and a \emph{separate longer-term extension}. Sections 1--3 are the original proposed challenge strands; Sections 4--7 are additional candidates, not extra commitments. The programme is data-focused and excludes theoretical modelling. A dataset's existence does not guarantee permission for Turing participants to use it.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Common preparation pack for participants}
The PI should prepare a short, consistent pack for whichever challenges are selected:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item A two-page question and background brief, including the minimum answer expected after five days.
\item Dataset inventory, definitions, access instructions, rights and disclosure rules.
\item One worked example, reproducible starter script and evaluation or quality criteria.
\item Suggested complementary teams, integration points and an end-of-week output format.
\item Known pitfalls, alternative methods and optional stretch goals.
\end{itemize}
A participant other than the PI should test access and run the starter workflow before the event. For licensed surveys, confirm individual permissions and approved hosting rather than assuming a PI-held download can be redistributed. A useful default is \emph{Team A: data construction/validation} and \emph{Team B: analysis/evaluation}, with joint integration and writing. These are suggestions: the Data Study Group researchers should have freedom to adapt their approach.
