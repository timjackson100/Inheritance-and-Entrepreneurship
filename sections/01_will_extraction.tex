\section{Strand 1: Extracting inheritance information from wills}
\label{sec:wills}

\subsection{Research question}
How accurately can an open-weight language model, adapted from US will-extraction benchmarks, identify people, family relationships, assets and intended bequests in wills from England and Wales?

The immediate data objective is a structured, source-grounded representation of what a will \emph{intends} to transfer. A bequest in a will is not evidence that a beneficiary received it.

\subsection{Data inventory}
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[IE4Wills (core benchmark)]
A published dataset of 45 authentic US wills and 16,018 human annotations covering testators, beneficiaries, assets, relationships and bequests. Its established annotation scheme permits a reproducible extraction baseline. Use is subject to the CC BY-NC 4.0 licence and an event-use rights check.
\item[Modern wills from England and Wales (conditional)]
A proposed, independently reviewed sample of 10--20 probate wills, subject to purchase, lawful processing, participant access, hosting and annotation. It tests transfer from US to UK drafting practice; it is too small to support representative claims about national extraction accuracy.
\end{description}

Scotland and Northern Ireland are separate potential extensions, not part of the initial UK-will benchmark.

\subsection{Target variables}
For each document, extract and preserve the relevant source passage for:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item the testator and any executors or trustees;
\item named beneficiaries and stated family or other relationships;
\item assets, specific gifts, residuary shares and conditional bequests;
\item gift conditions and contingent beneficiaries;
\item uncertainty, ambiguity, absent information and failed extractions.
\end{itemize}
The representation must distinguish a stated relationship from a relationship inferred by the model and must avoid inventing missing beneficiaries or transfers.

\subsection{Data and evaluation workflow}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Check the IE4Wills licence, obtain its documents and published data splits, and reproduce a conventional or published extraction baseline.
\item Define a consistent structured output schema, with evidence spans for every extracted assertion.
\item Obtain and review the permitted England-and-Wales sample, if approved; create independent reference labels.
\item Compare unadapted US-trained/prompted extraction with UK-specific prompts and schema adaptation. Fine-tuning is optional and requires enough authorised examples.
\item Score entity/relation precision, recall and F1, evidence-span accuracy, unsupported assertions, uncertainty and computational cost. Report US and UK performance separately.
\end{enumerate}

The published IE4Wills test split is small, so uncertainty should be reported at document level. Placeholder names in IE4Wills do not establish real-world identity-linkage accuracy.

\subsection{Expected outputs}
A documented extraction schema; clean benchmark and scoring scripts; runnable open-weight baselines; an error taxonomy; and a report on what UK-specific adaptation does and does not achieve.

\subsection{Preparation status and dependencies}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Available starting point:} public IE4Wills benchmark, subject to licence compliance.
\item \textbf{Not yet assured:} 10--20 lawfully usable UK wills and independently reviewed labels.
\item \textbf{Fallback:} report reproducible US benchmark results and the limitation on UK generalisation.
\end{itemize}
