\section{UK research stream C: Lifetime gifts, donor decisions and estates}
\label{sec:gifts}

The donor side matters because lifetime gifts, loans, trusts, inheritances and charitable legacies differ in timing, recipients and economic effects. IFS research by Boileau and Sturrock using WAS is a useful starting point.

\subsection{Candidate research questions and data}
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[C1. Lifetime gifts versus bequests]
How much is given before death, to whom and at what age? Need gifts \emph{given} and \emph{received}, amounts, dates, relationships, loans/repayments, donor wealth and eventual estate value. WAS is an initial source; question improvements under consultation are not existing observations.
\item[C2. Unequal division between children]
Are intended and actual estate shares equal across siblings? Need the number of children, gifts to each, signed final will/codicil, trusts, spouse transfers and final net distributions. An estate's gross value alone cannot establish recipient shares.
\item[C3. Care and late-life wealth depletion]
How do care needs, health, widowhood and longevity relate to asset sales, gifting and estates? Need repeated donor wealth, care status/costs, household changes, death dates and final estates. Wealth depletion alone does not reveal care spending.
\item[C4. Business succession]
How often are family businesses transferred in kind and what happens afterward? Need business ownership, tax reliefs, beneficiary relationships, post-transfer firm survival/investment and employees. Requires appropriate estate--firm linkage.
\item[C5. Tax and estate planning]
How do exemptions, thresholds and tax changes relate to gifting, trusts and estate composition? Need legislative timing and detailed tax/estate records. Non-taxable gifts may be absent from HMRC records.
\item[C6. Charitable and non-family bequests]
How much goes to charities, friends and other beneficiaries? Need will clauses, final accounts and possibly charity legacy receipts. Pledged gifts are not necessarily realised payments.
\end{description}

\subsection{Data and outputs}
A transfer-type dictionary should distinguish gifts, loans, inheritances, trusts, spousal transfers and business succession. Useful products include a donor/recipient variable inventory, a WAS descriptive giving-and-receiving panel, and a legally authorised estate sample comparing intended with realised distributions. Preserve selection, recall and possible double counting across lifetime gifts and estates.
